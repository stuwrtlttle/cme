A common language for defensive controls

Common Mitigation Enumeration

While CVE identifies specific vulnerabilities and CWE identifies classes of weaknesses, CME identifies the defensive controls that mitigate them.

A CME entry names a verifiable defensive control for an identified class of weaknesses and products. It records the control's primary effect (preventive, detective, or corrective), evidence maturity, operating conditions, verification, and typed relationships to other controls and frameworks. CVSS is CME's flagship scoring binding where applicable, not the membership test for a control.

How to use CME

Use CME to find candidate controls, then establish whether they apply and are actually active in your environment.

  1. Find a controlBrowse by tactic, search by name or ID, or start with a CWE. A relationship is a starting point, not proof that a control mitigates a specific vulnerability.
  2. Check applicabilityReview the product, platform, operating conditions, and control scope against the affected asset and vulnerability.
  3. Verify and assessConfirm the control is active and its conditions hold. Use the evidence state and any applicable framework binding to inform your assessment.

For scoring: CVSS impacts are optional and apply only when relevant to the vulnerability, the asset, and the control's documented conditions. A CME entry or product match alone does not establish a score reduction. Validate the result against the applicable CVSS version and assessment context.

How to read a CME entry

Entries describe the control, what it does, and the evidence and artifacts available to evaluate it. Not every entry contains every field.

Identity and organization

CME ID and description
A stable identifier, control name, and explanation of the defensive mechanism.
Tactic and category
The defensive objective and a more specific grouping. Tactics are D3FEND-aligned: Harden, Isolate, Detect, Evict, and Restore. CME adds Restore and currently excludes Deceive.
Function and layer
An optional function groups related controls, such as SELinux or seccomp. The layer identifies where the control operates: Network, OS/Kernel, Application, Data, or Identity.

Effect

The primary kind of outcome the control is intended to produce; this is distinct from how strong the evidence is.

Preventive
Blocks an attack or limits its ability to succeed or cause harm.
Detective
Identifies or signals an attack, suspicious activity, or a security-relevant condition.
Corrective
Removes a threat or repairs or restores systems after an incident.

Evidence state

Evidence describes how the control's effect can be credited, not whether it is enabled on your asset.

Deterministic
A verified preventive control whose conditions hold and whose CVSS-compatible effect is guaranteed. Examples include a correctly enforced noexec mount or a complete protocol disablement.
Quantified
Evidence-based, conditional effectiveness with a measured probability, supporting evidence, and operating conditions.
Unquantified
A relevant control with declared conditions but no measured efficacy. It is returned to humans and agents, but never changes an automated score.

For quantified effects, probability is conditional on the stated evidence and prerequisites; it is not a guarantee. Unquantified does not mean ineffective—it means the effect has not been measured well enough for automatic scoring credit.

Mappings and artifacts

CVSS vector impacts
Optional metric transitions and rationales for an applicable control. They describe potential environmental changes, not a new CVE base score. Evidence and conditions govern whether a transition may be credited.
CWE and framework relationships
Links to weakness classes and frameworks such as ATT&CK, D3FEND, and SCF. Typed relationships and rationales describe how the objects relate; a mapping is not proof of coverage in a particular deployment.
Verification
Methods and, where available, commands and expected results for checking whether the control is active on a particular platform. Review commands before running them.
Remediation
Optional platform-specific steps for enabling the control. Review preconditions, placeholders, restart requirements, reversibility, and operational impact before applying changes; the full JSON may contain safety details not shown on the entry page.
Applicability and platforms
Product and platform information, optionally using CVE-format product identifiers such as CPEs or package URLs. Applicability means the control can apply to a product—not that it is enabled or mitigates every vulnerability in it.
References
Sources and documentation supporting the control, its implementation, or its mappings. Consult the underlying evidence and conditions before relying on an effect.
